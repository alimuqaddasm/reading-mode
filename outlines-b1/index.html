Title

«اخلاق کیا ہے، اور دین میں اس کی جگہ» (the hub title; the mentor found the earlier wordplay title awkward).

The hook

The mirror on the way out of the house: hair set, the crease in the clothes checked, no step outside until the face is fit to be seen. Then the dua the Prophet صلی اللہ علیہ وآلہ وسلم taught Amir al-Muminin علیہ السلام for that very moment. The lesson's own addition, with Ali's permission; none of the four scholars quotes it. Promise: four questions: what this inner form is, how and where it forms, how far it reaches, and its place in religion.

The controlling image

The mirror: خَلق, the form the mirror shows (already made, given by God), and خُلق, the form no mirror shows (still being made, with our hand in it). It returns in every section as «اندر کی صورت», and at the close: the face in the mirror stays in the dust, the inner one goes with us, indeed ahead of us.

Sections

۱۔ ہم اپنے آپ کو کس چیز سے پہچانتے ہیں؟

  • Thought: the real "I" is inside; the body is the mount and the garment. So the inner form is the one that matters.
  • Argument: own: we know ourselves by what the mirror shows / AH: the madman of Egypt who tied a gourd to his foot so as not to get lost, and when it was moved to another man's foot asked «if he is me, who am I?» (AH, U5.2 P027:35-62, SE-S03; import) / own: the story is for looking at ourselves / AH: ninety nine in a hundred of us know ourselves by the gourd; the body is the gourd on the spirit's foot (P027:65-68) / AH: you are not the body, you ride it (P027:14-18) / AH: the body is the spirit's garment, loosened a little in sleep, taken off at death (P027:19-27) / own verdict: the one I call "I" is inside, and its form is خُلق, its plural اخلاق. «کدو کی دیکھ بھال پوری ہے، سوار کی خبر نہیں۔»
  • Narration: the mirror dua at the opening, «اللَّهُمَّ كَمَا حَسَّنْتَ خَلْقِي فَحَسِّنْ خُلُقِي» (Bihar 74:65 from Tuhaf al-Uqul p. 11; Jami 3550625).

۲۔ خُلق کیا ہے، اور کیسے بنتا ہے؟

  • Thought: خُلق is a trait that has taken root inside; it is built by repetition, so it is neither fate nor a day's work, and what was built can be rebuilt.
  • Argument: JS: خَلق and خُلق are one root, one seen by the eye, the other by insight (Raghib via Majlisi; U1.1 P008:2-8, 14-15) / JS: خُلق is a settled trait, good or bad (Majlisi; P007:4-9) / own definition test: if akhlaq meant one good act, anyone gentle once would be called good-natured; no one says so; one flare of anger is an act, a quick temper is a خُلق / AH: «انسان» from «اُنس»: man grows used to any world quickly (AH, U7.16 P095:1, 19, AZ-S04; import) with the young man who lost the taste for his mother's food, then lived on plain bread and found the same pleasure (P095:3-4, 12-18) / EZ: wake two weeks at one hour and it becomes a habit (U7.16 P016:140) / AH: habit makes the hardest thing easy: Amir al-Muminin علیہ السلام slept in the Prophet's place for three years in Shi'b Abi Talib, so the night of the Hijra was not new (U7.16 P001:17-21, 26, 30-33) / narration / EZ: so train your character to good; that is man's perfection (P016:15, 28) / EZ: the same door opens to evil: little by little the second nature becomes a wretched man's; leave the first craving at the first moment (U5.18 P025:18-22; import) / objection: what is spoilt stays spoilt? EZ: never say "I am used to it"; one who has choice can overcome any habit (U7.16 P019:57-60) / own verdict: خُلق is built by our hand and rebuilt by our hand.
  • Narration: Imam Muhammad Baqir علیہ السلام, «فَاحْضُرُونَا جَمِيعاً وَعَوِّدُوا أَنْفُسَكُمُ الْخَيْرَ وَكُونُوا مِنْ أَهْلِهِ تُعْرَفُوا» (Bihar 46:244 from al-Khara'ij; Jami 3501422), src EZ.

۳۔ یہ صورت کہاں بنتی ہے؟ دل میں

  • Thought: the inner form lives in the heart, which rules the body and keeps turning; hence God's commands reach the heart's own traits, not only the limbs.
  • Argument: narration (MM) / own: a bad eye leaves the ear working, but a spoilt heart spoils every limb; this is akhlaq's workshop / MM: the heart is called قلب because it turns from state to state (U5.8 P009:165; import) / own: that turning is hope and danger; work starts before a bad trait settles / EZ: some commands use the body (prayer, fasting, zakat, khums, jihad), others need no body: be sincere, be humble, do not be proud, selfish or envious; God tests by these too (U1.1 P005:6, 9-13) / own verdict: the smiling face is the fruit, not the root.
  • Narration: Imam Ja'far Sadiq علیہ السلام, «جَعَلَ الْقَلْبَ مُدَبِّراً لِلْجَسَدِ … وَإِنْ فَسَدَ الْقَلْبُ ذَهَبَتْ جَمِيعاً» (al-Risala al-Ihlilajiyya, in al-Dhari'a 1:481; Jami 4303915), src MM U5.2 P001:25 (import).

۴۔ اس صورت کا دائرہ کتنا بڑا ہے؟

  • Thought: the inner form shows wherever the heart deals with anyone: self, parents, neighbour, society, people, animals, plants, nature; and these are not on one level.
  • Argument: own: where the heart deals, the form shows / EZ: akhlaq is not only outward good temper and a smile; it is all these relations, not all on one level (U1.1 P004:4-5) / own verdict: the same form at home, in class, in the bazaar, alone; colours change, the form is one.
  • Narration: none. Teacher Copy: EZ's attribution of the list to Imam Sadiq علیہ السلام's words on «سنت قائمہ» (Ali chose to keep the narration in S08 and EZ's list as his own reading).

۵۔ دین میں اس کی جگہ

  • Thought: religion orders man's relation with his Creator, himself and others; it has three parts, beliefs, rulings and akhlaq; akhlaq is a full part, and whatever brings near to Paradise has been commanded, including what kind of person I become.
  • Argument: objection: is religion prayer and fasting, with akhlaq a decoration? / EZ: religion is the body of laws that makes God known and orders these relations (U1.2 P025:6-7) / EZ: Islam comes down to three parts (P013:32-35) / JS: what each part is (P005:14-19) / AH: the Prophet taught all three from the minbar; akhlaq is what reason itself knows good and ugly (P001:11) / own counterfactual: if akhlaq were outside religion it would not stand beside beliefs and rulings on the minbar / EZ: the Prophet gave all of Islam in one sentence (P003:3) / narration / EZ: this «anything» is not only fiqh: beliefs, akhlaq, our dealing with other creatures (P003:12) / own verdict: no خُلق is neutral.
  • Narration: the Prophet صلی اللہ علیہ وآلہ وسلم at the Farewell Pilgrimage, «مَا مِنْ شَيْءٍ يُقَرِّبُكُمْ مِنَ الْجَنَّةِ وَيُبَاعِدُكُمْ مِنَ النَّارِ إِلَّا وَقَدْ أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِهِ …» (al-Kafi 2:79 from Imam Baqir علیہ السلام; Jami 3263302), src EZ.

۶۔ یہ صورت ہم سے آگے آگے جاتی ہے

  • Thought: what we repeat becomes our form, and that form goes ahead of us and prepares our place.
  • Argument: narration (MM) / own: the deed is the servant who goes ahead to spread the bed / own close: the mirror dua is now understood: the face in the mirror stays in the dust, the inner one goes with us and before us / hand-off.
  • Narration: Imam Ja'far Sadiq علیہ السلام, «إِنَّ عَمَلَ الْمُؤْمِنِ يَذْهَبُ فَيَمْهَدُ لَهُ فِي الْجَنَّةِ كَمَا يُرْسِلُ الرَّجُلُ غُلَامَهُ فَيَفْرُشُ لَهُ» with 30:44 (Bihar 64:66 from Kitab al-Mu'min; Jami 3530440), src MM U9.12 P003:2 (import).

Practical step

Say the mirror dua each morning for a week; pick one recurring reaction (a خُلق, not a single act), note when and before whom it wakes, and try to stop at its first wave.

Dua

AH's closing dua: set right our bad akhlaq, adorn us with the Prophet's akhlaq, forgive our sins, grant us leaving sin to the end of life (U1.29 P005:1-4).

L02 «اچھائی اور برائی کو ہم کیسے پہچانتے ہیں؟»

Lesson file: lessons-v2/MA-B1-L02.md. Built from old S02 (whole) and old S03 sections 6 and 7. Build: 89 refs, 0 ref problems; verify: 2 ayat and 1 hadith, 0 FAIL. Handout 5 pages, Teacher Copy 14 pages (LibreOffice measure).

Title

«اچھائی اور برائی کو ہم کیسے پہچانتے ہیں؟» (the plain hub question, as Ali chose a plain title for L01).

The hook

An everyday moment the class knows: the phone screen turned over when an elder walks in, the subject changed when someone sits down, a thing done only when no one is looking. Then EZ's sharp form of it (import, U5.9, EZ-P86-0230): the young smoker who says his reason does not tell him to stop. Then why does he hide it from his parents, his principal, his friends? Lesson's own line: no one taught him it was a thing to hide and no book was open, yet his hand goes behind his back. So something inside him gave a verdict before he hid it. Link to L01's last question («اندر کی کون سی صورت خوبصورت ہے اور کون سی بدصورت، یہ ہم جانتے کیسے ہیں؟»): the answer is in the hiding. Promise: who the witness is, whom it lives in, what it judges, whether the age changes it, how much it asks, and why prophets are still needed.

The controlling image

«اندر کا گواہ», the witness inside who sees the inner form without a mirror (tying back to L01's mirror) and gives its verdict. It runs through every section heading («یہ گواہ کون ہے؟» and so on), is anchored in the Quran at 75:14-15 («بَلِ الْإِنسَانُ عَلَىٰ نَفْسِهِ بَصِيرَةٌ»), and is closed by the narration of Imam Musa al-Kazim علیہ السلام to Hisham («the inner proof is the intellects»), after which the smoker comes back once: the hand that went behind his back was the voice of God's inner proof.

Sections

۱۔ یہ گواہ کون ہے؟

  • Thought: the witness that judged is reason, which God placed in us as His proof; we know it by what it does: it tells ugly from beautiful.
  • Argument: MM first: reason is God's proof over man and His tongue inside him, so its guidance is God's guidance (MM, U1.3 P001:1, MMB_16_L017 ¶5; reworded plainly, his «معصوم بالذات» wording in the Teacher Copy) / AH: no logical definition is needed; we find reason by reason, and know it by its mark, telling ugly from beautiful (AH, P002:11-13, ML-S03) / AH: reason is what was not in us and came later: as children we did not know oppression; then one day we saw the strong crush the weak and said «نہ کرو، یہ ظلم ہے» (P002:14, 21-26) / AH: by it we know the virtues and vices of character; it is God's proof, His first prophet (P002:30-33) / own verdict: no one recited that sentence to us; God put the witness in. «اندر کا گواہ عقل ہے۔»
  • Narration: none. Teacher Copy: MM's exact wording; AH «reason is the inner tongue of God» (P005:13-15); AH on lying as a ruling of reason (P006:16).

۲۔ کیا یہ گواہ ہر ایک کے اندر ہے؟

  • Thought: the witness is in every human, of any land and any creed, and it speaks even against the one who makes excuses.
  • Argument: MM: the good and ugly that reason shows are known to every rational person by the light of his reason: oppression and seizing another's goods are ugly in themselves, kindness beautiful (MM, P001:15) / EZ: human fitra is one everywhere; ask anyone anywhere whether oppression is good (EZ, P008:10-13, TAR_0247) / EZ: is being cheated good, being tricked, being humiliated? everyone knows, unless propaganda has buried his fitra (P008:17-21) / own counterfactual: if knowing good and bad came only from books, the one who never read would be content to be cheated; it never happened anywhere / AH: the child who snatches a smaller child's sandwich says afterwards «I did a bad thing» (AH, P005:33-36, BJ-S08) / EZ: the proud, the miser, the slave of appetite pile up excuses, but looking at himself he knows it was he, and finds himself deserving blame; «as the Quran puts it» (EZ, P008:29-34) / ayah 75:14-15 / own verdict: excuses are made for those outside; the witness inside is not fooled.
  • Narration: ayah, al-Qiyama 75:14-15 (Tanzil, verified), src EZ P008:30 (transcript gap; see open question 2). Teacher Copy: JS «some things we would know even if religion had not said them» (P012:6-8).

۳۔ یہ گواہ کن باتوں پر گواہی دیتا ہے؟

  • Thought: the witness's field is exactly akhlaq; the Quran repeats what it already says.
  • Argument: MM: all the rulings of akhlaq go back to reason's rulings grounded in good and ugly (MM, P001:37) / MM: what is ugly to reason is ugly to God; so the sharia grounded many rulings in that ugliness: backbiting, tale-bearing, insult, pride, self-admiration, singing, sorcery, soothsaying, oppression, usurpation (MM, P009:16, MMB_17_L019) / own: this is a list of our own week (talking of someone absent, passing on words, laughing at a mistake, pride over marks); no fatwa was needed / MM: some acts are good or ugly in themselves and only the light of reason knows it; so the Book and Sunna command the good and forbid the ugly (MM, P004:1-2, MMB_17_L003) / ayah 16:90 / AH: not one of prayer's thousands of rulings comes from the Quran alone, but its akhlaq and what reason rules (lying and oppression forbidden, kindness binding) are plain from its words (AH, U1.9 P007:10-11, 29, MJ-S07; N1.9.3) / own verdict: «لعلکم تذکرون»: one reminds of what is already known; witness and Book speak one language.
  • Narration: ayah, al-Nahl 16:90 (Tanzil, verified), src MM P004:2. Teacher Copy: MM's full list and wording; JS «the Quran's signboard» (U1.9 P005:11-17).

۴۔ کیا زمانہ بدلنے سے یہ گواہی بدل جاتی ہے؟

۵۔ یہ گواہ ہم سے کتنا مانگتا ہے؟

  • Thought: the witness asks for good character in every instance, not once; its first and most shared demand is thanks to the Benefactor, in deed more than in word.

۶۔ جب اندر گواہ موجود ہے تو پیغمبر کس لئے؟

L03 «اخلاق نصیحت ہے یا حکم؟»

Lesson file: hub-planning/B01/lessons-v2/MA-B1-L03.md. Built from old S03 sections 1 to 5 (Fasl U1.14), with two imports from U8.18 (Bab 8) under policy P2. S03 sections 6 and 7 (U1.13, Kalila and Dimna, N1.22.6) belong to L02 and are not used here, not even in the Teacher Copy.

Title

«اخلاق نصیحت ہے یا حکم؟» (Is akhlaq advice, or a ruling?)

The hook

EZ's road parable (U8.18/P020:6-28, EZ-P73-0220, TAR_1258 ¶17-19): a driver turns across the solid line and a motorcyclist is hit. The police name two offences: the illegal turn (a fine only the law can waive) and the damage to the other man's vehicle (owed to him). Even if the state pardons every traffic fine that day, the damage is still owed. EZ's own reading: whoever owes people a right has done two wrongs, disobedience of God and harm to people.

Then the one-line link from L02 (the witness inside: is its verdict only advice, or law?), then the promise of the lesson (four questions).

The controlling image

The two accounts (دو حساب): God's account and the account of the person I dealt with. Opened by the road parable, used as the verdict of section 1 (both are accounts of one religion), the weighing of section 3 (cutting kin is written in both), the verdict of section 5 (paying one account is not a receipt for the other: the fine does not pay the damage, the damage does not pay the fine), and closed in section 6 by the bridge on the Path, with the last line «حساب دو ہیں، اور دونوں چکانے ہیں». The practical step is the same image on paper (two columns).

Sections

۱۔ کیا لوگوں والا حساب بھی دین کا حساب ہے؟

  • Thought: my dealings with people are not courtesy outside religion; they are one of religion's accounts, and in many places the heavier one.
  • Argument: 1. Common view: religion is God's account (prayer, fasting, hajj); dealings are manners. ({+}) 2. Islam is beliefs, devotional rulings and social rulings together; not beliefs alone, not practical rulings alone. (EZ, P015:4-7, N1.14.8) 3. One-line recall of L01's three parts. ({+}) 4. In many cases rulings on one's relation with others weigh more than some devotional rulings; devotions are often given for a social end. (EZ, P015:8-9, N1.14.8) 5. Counterfactual: what is not on the scale cannot outweigh anything; yet this pan is said to sink. ({+}) 6. Class examples: a promise to a classmate, rising for one's mother in exam days, the tone at home. ({+}) 7. Verdict: two accounts of one religion; the second sometimes heavier.
  • Narration: none. Old S03 section 1 (EZ P003:3-11, the Ghadir sentence and al-Ma'ida 5:3) goes to the Teacher Copy: L01 section 5 has taught it with the al-Kafi narration.

۲۔ پھر ہم اسے «صرف اخلاق» کہہ کر کیوں ٹال دیتے ہیں؟

  • Thought: «it is only akhlaq» is not an answer but a way out of the account.
  • Argument: 1. Why is the phrase so common? ({+}) 2. Relations with wife, children, neighbours and people are called moral matters, not important, left to ethics as advice, never given the weight of obligation that the recitation of prayer gets. (EZ, P014:8 and P014:10-12, N1.14.7; who says it, «many scholars and Islamologists» P014:9, is in the Teacher Copy) 3. Definition test: if akhlaq meant only advice, there would be neither promise nor threat. ({+}) 4. Are moral matters part of Islam? Then do they not carry glad tidings, warning and threat, and a question on the Day before other matters, with the Fire for no answer? (EZ, P013:3-4, N1.14.6) 5. Then should they not stand with the practical rulings and beside the beliefs, not be passed over with one word? (EZ, P013:5-6, N1.14.6) 6. Objection voiced and answered with a question: «a sharp remark, a forgotten promise are small things»; what is asked before other matters on the Day is not small. ({+}) 7. Verdict.
  • Narration: none.

۳۔ کیا ان باتوں پر واقعی آگ کی وعید ہے؟

  • Thought: kinship and kindness to parents are rulings of the sharia with the Fire behind them, not good counsel to postpone in a quarrel.
  • Argument: 1. Raising the question is not enough; how does the sharia rank these things? Take kinship. ({+}) 2. Some think joining kin is a moral reminder; no, it is a sharia ruling, and cutting kin makes one of the Fire. (EZ, P010:14-16, N1.14.3) 3. Skipping night prayer sends no one to the Fire; kinship, parents and honouring the guest are within God's rulings. (EZ, P010:17-19, N1.14.3) 4. Are our moral discussions not sharia rulings? Is a sharia question only about tayammum? (EZ, P010:24-25, N1.14.3) 5. Comparison for students: anxious over a condition of tayammum, untroubled by years of silence with a cousin or uncle. ({+}) 6. Parents: ignoring their word, raising the voice, which we shrink as «a home matter». ({+}) 7. Verdict: cutting kin is written in both accounts.
  • Narration: none (Majlisi's count of 110 narrations is EZ's own claim and goes to the Teacher Copy).

۴۔ یہ حساب کہاں تک پھیلا ہوا ہے؟

  • Thought: the people's account reaches the neighbour, the tongue and the phone; mockery born of envy is an open debt.
  • Argument: 1. If kinship is so, where do other relations stand? Begin with the neighbour. ({+}) 2. Narration «ما آمن بي…». (EZ, P011:5-6, N1.14.4) 3. Faith is denied, not just manners; if it were only etiquette, what would faith have to do with it? ({+}) 4. Pride, conceit and envy while in the heart are not held against one; envy acted out in mocking, making faces and harming a believer is another matter. (EZ, P011:9-11, N1.14.4, softened; the hadith of raf' and the question of 'adala in the Teacher Copy) 5. Waste, extravagance, neglect of parents and cutting kin are in the text of Islam. (EZ, P011:13-14, N1.14.4) 6. Today's form: imitating a classmate, a laughing line under a photo, a group message that belittles; the laugh ends, the account stays open. ({+}) 7. Verdict.
  • Narration: «مَا آمَنَ بِي مَنْ بَاتَ شَبْعَانَ وَجَارُهُ جَائِعٌ», Imam al-Baqir علیہ السلام from the Prophet صلی اللہ علیہ وآلہ وسلم; al-Kafi 2, Bab haqq al-jiwar, h 14 (Jami 3265477, unvowelled); text from Wasa'il al-Shi'a 8, Bab 88 (Jami 3356892), same chain, vowelled. Bab 1, not an import.

۵۔ الٹی غلطی: «اصل چیز تو اخلاق ہے»

  • Thought: the opposite error closes God's account in the name of good character; good akhlaq is no substitute for prayer and halal and haram.
  • Argument: 1. The other face: people who keep the people's account and think God's is settled by it. ({+}) 2. «We have two sons-in-law: one does not pray but has good akhlaq, the other prays but is ill-tempered.» Can one who does not do his duty to God be a good man? Lesson's reading: kin were gathered for the last word, and the word was prayer; kinship does not replace prayer. ({+}) 6. Support: «we try to have good akhlaq, that is the spirit of religion; halal, haram, hijab do not matter much»; never. (JS, P002:14-18, N1.14.1, support only) 7. Verdict with the image: the fine does not pay the damage, the damage does not pay the fine.
  • Narration: «… اجْمَعُوا لِي كُلَّ مَنْ بَيْنِي وَبَيْنَهُ قَرَابَةٌ … إِنَّ شَفَاعَتَنَا لَا تَنَالُ مُسْتَخِفّاً بِالصَّلَاةِ», Bihar al-Anwar 47:2 from Thawab al-A'mal, Umm Hamida's report (Jami 3501895). Bab 1 (EZ reads it), not an import.

۶۔ دونوں حساب کہاں چکتے ہیں؟

  • Thought: neither account can be dropped; the people's account has its last checkpoint on the Path.
  • Argument: 1. Both errors side by side. ({+}) 2. Confining religion to beliefs is wrong. (EZ, P003:38-40) 3. Holding only the rituals is deficient; both without akhlaq is still deficient; the Prophet spoke on all three. (EZ, P003:41-43) 4. Back to the road: a general pardon leaves the damage standing; here a court collects it; where is its checkpoint in the hereafter? ({+}) 5. Final narration (below). 6. On that bridge: the relative not spoken to, the neighbour who slept hungry, the classmate mocked before all. ({+}) 7. Closing: what we called «only akhlaq» is a ruling, and its last checkpoint is on the Path. «حساب دو ہیں، اور دونوں چکانے ہیں.» ({+})
  • Narration: Imam al-Sadiq علیہ السلام on al-Fajr 89:14 (inline ayah, Tanzil checked): «قَنْطَرَةٌ عَلَى الصِّرَاطِ لَا يَجُوزُهَا عَبْدٌ بِمَظْلِمَةٍ». L04 «ہم اس دنیا میں کیوں ہیں، اور آدمی کو آدمی کیا بناتا ہے»

Title

«ہم اس دنیا میں کیوں ہیں، اور آدمی کو آدمی کیا بناتا ہے»

The lesson's single question, put plainly. «آدمی» rather than «انسان» in the title so that it already carries the controlling image.

The hook

An everyday sentence almost every student has heard at home: a father or mother saying in anger «تو آدمی نہیں بنے گا». Everyone knows it does not mean «you will not get a degree». Then the poem EZ recites to young people (told neutrally, «ایک پرانی نظم میں»): the angry father, the son who leaves, becomes governor and rich, sends for his father, looks him over with pride and asks «do you recognise me? you said I would never become a man; now see my grandeur». The story stops there: «what the old man answered, we hear at the end of the lesson».

Then the one-line link from L03 («if my dealings with people are law and every word is reckoned, why was man placed in this world at all?») and the promise: why we are here, what makes a human human, and who stays human in form but falls below the beast.

Source: EZ P035:17-29 (TAR_0407, cleanest telling of the poem; same poem in P033, P034, P037).

AH's Friday story (AH-P10-0105, the menu's first choice) is not the hook: AH tells it from a Sunni source (Sha'rani, Lawaqih al-Anwar) as the opening of a story of meeting the Imam of the Age. It sits in the Teacher Copy after section 1, with a «!!» note, only the Friday scene and the thought «ما لهذا خلقنا».

The controlling image

«آدمی بننا، حاکم بننا نہیں»: becoming human, not becoming a ruler. It opens the lesson (the son's boast), runs under every section (each section answers what «becoming human» really is: not eating and living; being cleansed of envy here; thanks and care for others; the manner of the shared act; not falling below the beast), and closes it (the father's answer, then the narration of Imam al-Kazim علیہ السلام that names exactly what the son lacked: kindness to parents, leaving conceit and pride).

Sections

۱۔ کیا ہم کھانے اور جینے کے لئے لائے گئے ہیں؟

  • Thought: we were not brought here to eat, grow fat and die; we were made for servanthood and nearness. Eating, sleeping, earning are provision for the road, not the destination.
  • Argument: 1. The plain question: most of my day is study, earning, eating, sleeping. Is that the purpose? (lesson) 2. Not brought here to eat, grow fat, multiply and die; for that the cow and the donkey were brought (AH, U1.4 P008:4-5). 3. If that were all, sheep and kids would be better than us: they graze better and are freer (AH, P009:7-10). 4. Counterfactual lock: if man were made only to eat and live, the best eater would be the most successful and we would remember the dead by their table; we never do. We remember truthfulness, loyalty, goodness (lesson). 5. 51:56, then: we were brought to serve God, so that by servanthood our degrees of nearness rise and the spirit is perfected (AH, P008:6). 6. The original aim of creation is nearness to God (EZ, P015:4). 7. Verdict: provision, not destination. The destination is nearness.
  • Ayah: الذاریات 51:56 (Tanzil).

۲۔ یہ کام اسی دنیا میں کیوں؟

  • Thought: this world, with its illness, want and failure, is the only place where a person can be cleansed of envy, rancour and deceit; knowing my own need makes me humble, and a humble heart does not burn at another's blessing.
  • Argument: 1. Objection raised: illness, want, failure, people who overtake us; are these not obstacles? (lesson) 2. MM first: it is impossible for man to be perfected, know his Lord and have complete joy free of envy, rancour, deceit, rivalry and jostling, and know his measure and limit, except by coming into this world (MM, P001:88). 3. Full delight only after tasting the opposite: who never saw poverty does not taste plenty (MM, P036:8). 4. So God set people on earth and held them here, to know themselves, their helplessness and their need, and be purified of foul traits (MM, P036:10). 5. Love of God needs knowing one's helplessness; one sees it when in need of others and ill; then he knows his measure, claims nothing not his, does not lord it over others (MM, P036:5). 6. Who knows and loves his Lord knows His chosen servants and never envies them (MM, P036:6). 7. Concrete: the one who never fell behind smiles at the one who did; a week of illness teaches the worth of health and how to visit the sick (lesson). 8. Objection voiced and answered with a question: «then the world is a punishment?» Is a bitter medicine given to heal called a punishment? (lesson) 9. Who purified himself became fit; who neglected wronged himself, not his Lord, who added messengers who teach good character and warn of bad (MM, P036:11). 10. Verdict: this world is where the inner form is washed.
  • No narration (MM's own prose, Urdu only).

۳۔ پھر آدمی اور جانور میں فرق کہاں ہے؟

  • Thought: the animal also eats, sleeps and raises young; the human differs not in stature but in feeling, akhlaq and thanks, and in making another's trouble his own task.
  • Argument: 1. The puzzle: same acts, so what makes a human human? (lesson) 2. The well-fed donkey eats the weak one's handful and kicks him (AH, P006:4). 3. Man is not so; the difference is not stature but feelings, states of the spirit, akhlaq, virtues of the self (AH, P006:5-6). 4. No donkey thanks another; man says the benefactor's right must be known (AH, P006:7-9). 5. Animals gather and scream round a dying crow or a threatened nest, then go back to their business (EZ, P007:172-176). 6. «Here the human row parts from the animal row» (EZ, P007:177-178); for the human, another's trouble becomes his task (lesson). 7. The narration, then EZ's morning questions: whose house, whose need, whose child, whom did you guide? (EZ, P007:180-184). 8. Concrete: a classmate absent for days; for the animal no news, for the human a question (lesson). 9. Verdict: a human is known not by his form but by remembering the giver and caring for others.
  • Narration: «مَنْ أَصْبَحَ لَا يَهْتَمُّ بِأُمُورِ الْمُسْلِمِينَ فَلَيْسَ بِمُسْلِمٍ», al-Kafi 2:163 h 1 (Jami 3263716), Imam al-Sadiq علیہ السلام from the Prophet صلی اللہ علیہ وآلہ وسلم; src EZ P007:180. (Old S04 cited al-Wafi because the script could not reach al-Kafi; it now can.)

۴۔ جو کام جانور بھی کرتا ہے، وہ آدمی کا کیسے بنتا ہے؟

  • Thought: the shared acts become human acts by manner and intention; a glass of water can be a human's or an animal's.
  • Argument: 1. Question: do the shared acts stay the animal's share? (lesson) 2. Religion does not fight natural needs: food, water, sleep are our nature (EZ, U1.3 P031:7-10). 3. It raises what is too little, moderates what is too much, orders what is loose (EZ, U1.3 P031:13-16). 4. Then: do the same natural act with the intention of nearness, and rise through it (EZ, U1.3 P031:19-21). (N1.3.10) 5. The animal drinks when thirsty; the human's drinking, tea and food differ (EZ, U1.27 P004:37-39). 6. Bismillah over each cup, alhamdulillah after, care that it is not haram, impure or taken from someone; the sheep grazes wherever it reaches (EZ, U1.27 P004:41-44). (N1.27.2) 7. Religion has given manners for eating, walking, sitting and social dealings (JS, U1.27 P003:5-8; support only). 8. Definition test: if humanity meant only the human shape, no one would remark on how someone eats; but at a wedding meal, when one breaks the queue, fills a plate and leaves half, and says no word of thanks, we say at once «what a way to eat» (lesson). 9. Verdict: the act stays, the manner changes, and the human station lies in the manner.
  • No narration.

۵۔ اور جو یہ فرق کھو دے؟

  • Thought: since humanity lies in akhlaq, it can be lost: the form stays human while the inside turns; the same akhlaq can lift a person above the angel or drop him below the beast.
  • Argument: 1. If humanity is manner and akhlaq, it can slip away (lesson). 2. MM first: God's light is for the intelligent as intelligent, not for one fouler than the beasts: who disobeys his nature, neglects his reason, does not avoid what his nature tells him to avoid, is proud, stubborn, denies, and wrongs God's servants like a beast of prey (MM, P005:2). 3. Concrete: nothing in that list belongs only to tyrants; the stubborn refusal to admit a mistake, mocking the weaker, taking his share; what the fat donkey did, done to a weak classmate (lesson; ties back to section 3). 4. Import image: man kneaded of clay and spirit, a share of the angel and of the lower pulls; Sa'di's verse, the strange compound of angel and animal: leave the one and pass the angel, leave the other and fall below the beast (EZ, U5.3 P025:136-142). 5. Two lives: the animal life by which we eat and speak; the meaningful life, without which we are all dead (EZ, P013:12-14, 31-33). 6. 8:24, then: we are alive by the animal life; the real human life is the life of faith, knowledge and purity (EZ, P013:37-40). 7. Verdict: a person is human by his akhlaq, not his form; it can take him above the angel or below the beast.
  • Ayah: الانفال 8:24 (Tanzil).

۶۔ آدمی بننا، حاکم بننا نہیں

  • Thought: becoming human is not becoming a ruler, rich or learned; nearness to God comes by kindness to parents and by leaving envy, conceit and pride. We were placed here to become human.
  • Argument: 1. Back to the old father before his son (lesson). 2. The answer: «I did not say you would not become a ruler; I said you would not become a man, my dear son», and «you still have not» (EZ, P034:20-23, P033:20). 3. Definition test: if «becoming a man» meant getting ahead, the father was proved wrong and the son had won; yet the old man laughed: one word, two meanings (lesson). 4. A man may get a degree, become an engineer, a scholar, rich, a minister, a lawyer, and not be the man God wants (EZ, P033:22-23). 5. Not «do not become rich or a preacher», but «what kind of preacher?»; the mother-in-law who swore she would never treat her daughter-in-law as she was treated (EZ, P035:42-48). 6. Becoming learned is a stage of taqwa; becoming human is its condition (EZ, P037:22-24). 7. The mechanic's sign: we fly better than birds and swim better than fish, but do not walk the earth like one who has become human (JS, P031:22, 28-29; support only). 8. What the son lacked was not a skill or a post: he looked at his father with self-love, and sent for him instead of going (lesson); so when position comes, go to your father, do not summon him (EZ, P034:5-6, the poem's own frame). 9. The final narration, laid against the son: city, wealth, court; but no kindness to his parent, and conceit and pride had become his name (lesson). 10.   • Narration: «يَا هِشَامُ أَفْضَلُ مَا يَتَقَرَّبُ بِهِ الْعَبْدُ إِلَى اللَّهِ بَعْدَ الْمَعْرِفَةِ بِهِ الصَّلَاةُ وَبِرُّ الْوَالِدَيْنِ وَتَرْكُ الْحَسَدِ وَالْعُجْبِ وَالْفَخْرِ», Tuhaf al-'Uqul p. 391 (Jami 3739571), Imam al-Kazim علیہ السلام to Hisham; src JS P014:7 (JS also reads it in P011:1-2).   • The single question, in L04's own closing words (lessons-v2/MA-B1-L04.md, last line): «اگر میں یہاں آدمی بننے کے لئے ہوں، تو یہ دنیا میرے لئے کیا ہے، اور اس میں کون سی چیز ہے جو کبھی نہیں بیچی جا سکتی؟»

2. The opening moment, and the link from L04

  • Opening moment: the ice seller in the summer street, crying «my capital is melting». EZ tells it (from Muhammad Taqi Shariati, through a boy) while opening al-Saff 61:10, the trade that saves: a man's life is that ice; youth and health go before one notices; in this trade we hand over life, youth and means, so what do we take back? EZ-P31-0001, TAF_0047 ¶2 to 5, U9.1 (Bab 9 part 1, row A1; story list EZ 9.49.1). IMPORT, Bab 9. Told neutrally («ایک واقعہ ملتا ہے»), the conclusion in the lesson's voice.
  • Link from L04 (one line, the lesson's voice): L04 ended on «then what is this world to me, and what in it must never be sold?»; the ice seller answers half of it before the lesson begins: the world is a market, and something of mine is already on the counter.

3. The controlling image, and its return

  • Image: the market (بازار اور سودا). Life is the capital, and it melts whether it is sold or not; the world is the market where it is spent; there is one Buyer and one price; and one thing is never put on the counter.
  • Carried through: section 1 (we are passing through the market, not living in it), section 2 (who buys, and for what), section 3 (the one thing Amir al-Muminin علیہ السلام would not trade even for his life), section 4 (Asiya refuses the palace as a price), section 5 (the opposite mistake: religion priced by what it earns).
  • Return at the close: the final narration names the only price the self may be sold for; the lesson's last line goes back to the ice: it melts in any case; the only choice left is what it buys.

3. The controlling image, and how it returns

Image: پرہیز, the patient's regimen, the part of the cure that is in the patient's own hand. The prescription can be perfect; without پرہیز it does nothing. The Urdu word already carries the lesson: پرہیز and پرہیزگاری.

3. The controlling image, and how it returns

Image: gold did not make itself gold (EZ, EZ-P71-0005 passage, gather U1.17 P024:39 to 43). Gold is dearer than silver; neither chose. What one is given is real worth; what one does with it is another kind of worth.

Where it returns:

  • Section 1: gold and silver; the quick student and the slow one; neither made himself.
  • Section 2: before God only what is done by choice is weighed; the gold one was given rises only by what one does with it.
  • Section 3: God weighs the metal under the shine: the intention.
  • Section 4: gold carried and not used is a heavier load; nine generations of «gold» fathers save no one.
  • Close: Imam al-Hadi علیہ السلام seats a jurist above men born to the noblest lineage: the gold of birth set below the gold earned.

4. Sections

۱۔ جو ملا ہے، کیا وہ کچھ بھی نہیں؟

Thought: knowledge, beauty, health, a good family are real excellences; they are not to be denied, and they were given, not made.

Argument:

  1. Why is gold dearer than silver? Did gold make itself gold, or silver make itself less? Still gold is worth more; and God did not make all things alike, and this difference is not against His justice. EZ, EZ-P71-0005, TAR_1184 ¶29 (gather P024:28 to 43 and 64); N1.17.6 and N1.17.7, U1.17; Bab 1. EZ's thesis line from the same record: «منکر ارزش‌ها در نظام ارزشی ما نباشیم».
  2. The same holds among people: one is born handsome, one is not; one is born with the mind of Ibn Sina, one stays slow after fifty years; neither made himself so, and they are not equal: «هل يستوي الذين يعلمون والذين لا يعلمون». Same passage, P024:103 to 110; Bab 1.
  3. Such an excellence (EZ's own term «فضیلت») belongs to its holder however it came; this holds in stones, plants, animals and men. EZ, EZ-P35-0135, TAR_0093 ¶7 to 8; N1.17.10, U1.17; Bab 1. The Handout teaches the thing, not the term: «جو ملا» against «جو کمایا» (menu question 10).

۲۔ پھر خدا کے ہاں کیا گنا جاتا ہے؟

Thought: before God only what a person does by his own choice is weighed, and that is taqwa; so the door is open to everyone.

Argument:

  1. In every being there are two things: the excellence it has, given or earned, and the worth before God that comes from what it does by choice. Thought: what God weighs lies under the deed: the intention. He weighs what I would be if I stayed.

۱۔ «ایمان» کا لفظ کیا کہتا ہے؟

Thought: iman is not a badge one wears but a place one is in: the shelter of God, entered where there is danger.

Argument:

  1. The root of iman and mu'min is amn: a place without danger; iman is going into the shelter.
